1.7 Explain the two compilation phases of Java programs. 
The two compilation phases of Java programs are:
1. Compilation – The Java compiler (javac) converts the source code (.java) into bytecode (.class).
2. Execution – The Java Virtual Machine (JVM) loads and executes the bytecode, translating it into machine code that the computer can understand.


1.8 One of the world’s most common objects is a wrist watch. Discuss how each of the following terms and concepts applies to the notion of a watch: object, attributes, behaviors, class, inheritance (consider, for example, an alarm clock), modeling, messages, encapsulation, interface and information hiding.
Answers>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>
A wristwatch can be explained using object-oriented programming concepts as follows:
•	Object: A wristwatch is an object because it is a specific thing with its own properties and functions.
•	Attributes: These are the characteristics of a watch, such as time, color, size, brand, and battery level.
•	Behaviors: These are the actions a watch can perform, such as displaying time, setting an alarm, starting a stopwatch, and changing the time.
•	Class: A watch class is a blueprint that defines the common attributes and behaviors of all watches.
•	Inheritance: An alarm clock can inherit features from a basic clock, such as displaying time, and add its own feature, such as an alarm.
•	Modeling: Modeling represents the important features and behaviors of a watch in a simplified form for designing a program.
•	Messages: Messages are instructions sent to an object, such as telling a watch to set the time or turn on the alarm.
•	Encapsulation: Encapsulation combines the watch's data and methods into one unit and controls how they can be accessed.
•	Interface: The buttons or touchscreen of a watch form its interface, allowing the user to interact with it.
•	Information hiding: The internal workings of the watch, such as how it calculates and stores time, are hidden from the user.


1.9 (Test-Drive: Carbon Footprint Calculator) 
Some scientists believe that carbon emissions, especially from the burning of fossil fuels, contribute significantly to global warming and that this can be combatted if individuals take steps to limit their use of carbon-based fuels. Organizations and individuals are increasingly concerned about their “carbon footprints.” Websites such as TerraPass http://www.terrapass.com/carbon-footprint-calculator/ and Carbon Footprint http://www.carbonfootprint.com/calculator.aspx provide carbon-footprint calculators. Test-drive these calculators to determine your carbon footprint. Exercises in later chapters will ask you to program your own carbon-footprint calculator. To prepare for this, use the web to research the formulas for calculating carbon footprints. 
Answers>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>>
A carbon footprint calculator estimates the amount of greenhouse gases, mainly carbon dioxide (CO₂), produced by a person’s activities.
To calculate a carbon footprint, information such as the following is usually considered:
•	Electricity and gas usage at home.
•	Fuel used by cars and other vehicles.
•	Air travel.
•	Other energy and fuel consumption.
The general formula is:
Carbon footprint = Amount of fuel or energy used × Emission factor
The result is usually expressed in kilograms or tonnes of CO₂ equivalent (CO₂e).
These calculations help individuals understand their environmental impact and identify ways to reduce carbon emissions.


1.10 Summary
Body Mass Index (BMI) is a measure used to estimate whether a person has a healthy body weight for their height.
The formula is:
BMI = weight (kg) ÷ [height (m)]²
For example, if a person weighs 70 kg and is 1.75 m tall:
BMI = 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 22.86
Common BMI ranges for adults are:
•	Below 18.5 — Underweight
•	18.5–24.9 — Normal weight
•	25.0–29.9 — Overweight
•	30.0 and above — Obese
BMI is a screening measure and does not directly measure body fat.

1.12 Gender Neutrality
A simple procedure would be:
1.	Read the paragraph from beginning to end.
2.	Look for gender-specific words such as “wife,” “husband,” “man,” “woman,” “son,” and “daughter.”
3.	Replace each word with its gender-neutral replacement:
o	wife/husband → spouse
o	man/woman → person
o	son/daughter → child
4.	Continue until the entire paragraph has been checked.
5.	Read the paragraph again to make sure the replacements are correct.
A strange word like “incubate” could be created if the program replaces parts of words instead of complete words. For example, if it replaces “woman” inside another word, it may accidentally create an incorrect word.
This step-by-step procedure is called an algorithm.
